# Design

Ledger, not dashboard. The app descends from a spreadsheet and its job is columns of
figures that have to be read exactly, so the layout is hairlines and vertical rhythm
rather than cards: amounts sit in a right-aligned tabular column, a serif carries
labels and names, and red is reserved for negatives the way a paper ledger uses red
ink.

**Charts are grey lines with a dot per month**, not filled areas or coloured series.
Each line is labelled at its right end, so identity comes from the name rather than
the shade, which also means colourblindness cannot make two loans
indistinguishable. The ink ramp exists only to keep neighbouring lines apart and was
validated as an ordinal ramp against both papers: monotone lightness, visible step
gaps, and the lightest step clearing the surface.

A loan that has already cleared is not labelled, since it sits flat on zero where a
label would collide with the axis and repeat what the legend says.

**Quiet actions are links, not buttons.** Correcting the balance and skipping a
month's buffer read as the closing clause of the sentence that prompted them. A
boxed control beside an italic aside lines up with nothing, and both are rare,
secondary things to want.

**Nothing is stated that is not known.** A household with no data shows no figure
rather than a confident zero, and the app asks one question at a time until it can
answer its own. Each question disappears by being answered, never by being
dismissed, and the reward for answering is the figure above it changing.

**Empty sections explain themselves** while they are empty, in one or two sentences,
and stop once there is anything in them. If one needs a third sentence, the section
is too complicated and that is the real problem.
